Yessss. And honestly, this is a very good project idea for you, especially because it solves a real problem in your own house rather than being another generic CRUD app.

The important thing is that we should not over-engineer it. Since you want to live-code it quickly and actually have your mom use it, I would build it as a rule-based personalized meal recommendation system, not an ML model. The intelligence will come from the data model + scoring algorithm.

One important correction first: I would not encode a rule such as "eating too many carbs causes urea in your knees." That's not a sound nutritional relationship. Instead, the app can detect things like repeated meals, high/low protein or fiber patterns, excessive repetition, and nutrient gaps. WHO's current guidance emphasizes adequacy, balance, moderation and diversity, with varied vegetables, fruits, legumes, nuts, whole grains and protein sources.

1. The actual idea

I would define the app as:

A personalized Pakistani meal planner that answers "Aaj kya banaun?" by considering what the family likes, what was eaten recently, nutritional balance, meal repetition, and complementary foods.

That's much more interesting than:

"A website containing Pakistani recipes."

The core loop is:

Mom opens app → sees today's recommendations → chooses a dish → app records it → nutrition is calculated → app suggests complementary foods → tomorrow's recommendations change based on what was eaten.

That's the entire product.

And I would make "Aaj kya banaun?" the home screen, not hide it inside a dashboard.

2. The most important architectural decision
Don't build an AI/ML recommendation model initially.

You don't need TensorFlow, Python ML, embeddings, vector databases, or an LLM.

You can get a surprisingly intelligent system using:

Food database
      ↓
Nutritional information
      ↓
User preferences
      ↓
Recent meal history
      ↓
Nutrient gaps
      ↓
Food repetition
      ↓
Compatibility / pairing
      ↓
Recommendation score
      ↓
Top 3–6 dishes

For example:

Chicken Pulao
Score: 87

Chicken Biryani
Score: 81

Mutton Karahi
Score: 78

Chana Dal
Score: 64

Bhindi
Score: 59

But don't show "87" to your mom.

Internally it's a score.

UI says:

⭐ Recommended for today

Chicken Pulao
چکن پلاؤ

"Good protein + energy. You haven't had it recently."

That's much more natural.

That's exactly what I recommend for this project.

4. The recommendation formula

We can eventually define something like:

Recommendation Score =
    Preference Score
  + Favorite Bonus
  + Nutritional Balance Score
  + Variety Score
  + Nutrient Gap Score
  + Pairing Score
  - Recent Meal Penalty
  - Repetition Penalty
  - Dislike Penalty

For example:

Chicken Biryani

Preference:          +25
Favorite:            +15
Nutritional balance: +12
Variety:             +10
Nutrient gap:         +8
Recently eaten:       -5
Repetition:           -2

TOTAL = 63

You can tweak these weights later.

This is much easier to develop and debug than ML.

And importantly, you can explain why something was recommended.

14. The REALLY important thing: portion sizes

This is where nutritional calculations become difficult.

"Chicken Biryani" doesn't have one universal nutrition value.

One person's biryani might have:

more oil
more rice
less chicken

Someone else's:

more chicken
less oil

So don't pretend:

Chicken Biryani = exactly 645 calories.

Instead define:

Standard serving

For example:

1 medium serving / approximately X grams

Then your UI says:

Estimated nutrition per serving

That word estimated is important.

For a household app, that's perfectly reasonable.

15. Don't try to manually invent every micronutrient

This is where I'd use reliable nutrition data as the foundation rather than asking an LLM:

"Hey Gemini, tell me the magnesium in Pakistani chicken biryani."

LLMs can generate plausible-looking numbers that aren't reliable enough for this purpose.

We can use established nutrition databases as the underlying reference and adapt recipes to your Pakistani dishes. For example, USDA's FoodData Central is designed around nutrient data for foods.

For your first version, though, I would actually make a curated dataset of perhaps 40–60 dishes and manually verify the values.

That's much faster than building some giant nutrition platform.

46. One important nutritional principle for our engine

I would make the recommendation engine optimize for patterns, not individual "healthy/unhealthy" labels.

For example:

Yesterday:
Rice + chicken

Today:
Dal + roti + vegetable

Tomorrow:
Mutton + salad

That's more sensible than:

Rice = bad
Chicken = good
Dal = good

A healthy diet is about overall adequacy, balance, moderation and diversity, and WHO specifically notes that dietary composition should also reflect cultural context and individual characteristics.

So our algorithm should basically ask:

"Given what this person has eaten recently, what would make today's overall pattern more varied and balanced while still respecting what they actually like to eat?"

That is the fundamental intelligence of the app.
